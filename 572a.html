Reading: Young Astronomers in Buenos Aires

My name is Tomás Ríos, I am 17 years old, and I was born in Almagro, Buenos Aires, where I still live with my sister. I love stargazing and building telescopes, and I am very good at astrophysics. Next month, my science club and I are visiting the Galileo Galilei Planetarium to present our research. We have observed Jupiter's moons for five months, and we have already taken over two hundred high-resolution photographs.

Last year, light pollution in the city made our observations very difficult. Therefore, we decided to design a digital filter for amateur telescopes. While we were testing our lens on the school roof, an engineer from CONAE (Argentina's Space Agency) visited us and donated a professional camera. If more schools join our network, students will collect better astronomical data across the country. However, our teacher always says: "You won't discover anything new unless you check your calculations twice."

Nowadays, our space photos are shared by science blogs every week, and new satellite models are being built by our team. In my opinion, astronomy is the most fascinating science I have ever studied, and it is much more exciting than reading textbooks. Tomorrow morning, I am meeting an astrophysicist to analyze our latest images. I am sure Argentina is going to launch even more advanced satellites in the near future!
